DJI Osmo Pocket 4P vs Insta360 Luna Ultra: Ultimate 2026 Travel Setup?

I took both 2026 flagship pocket gimbal cameras out around Uluwatu — filming through luxury villas, dark night walks, and windy Melasti Beach — with one rule: I’m keeping one and returning the other. Here’s the full breakdown from our real-world test.

Camera I Kept Insta360 Luna Ultra Detachable screen-as-a-mic, 8K flexibility & modular travel kit
Best Value Pick DJI Osmo Pocket 4P Stronger price-to-performance & classic pocketable simplicity
Jump Directly to Any Test

Full Written Review

1. Real-World Travel Testing — Not a Local Park

Most gimbal comparisons happen on a calm afternoon walk through a park, which tells you almost nothing about filming actual holidays and hotel reviews. To properly separate the DJI Osmo Pocket 4P and the Insta360 Luna Ultra, we ran them side by side across a full tour of Akula Bingin Villa in Bali, bright midday glare, dimly lit evening streets, and the windy shoreline at Melasti Beach — testing both the main 1x lens and the 3x telephoto lens along the way.

2. The Standout Feature: Detachable Screen That Doubles as a Wireless Mic

If there’s one feature that genuinely changes how you film solo or as a couple while traveling, it’s the Luna Ultra’s detachable OLED screen. Down on a noisy beach with crashing waves and beach-club music, you can pop the screen straight off the handle and hold it discreetly in your hand as a wireless microphone and live monitor — without wiring up a separate clip-on mic or looking obvious in a hotel lobby. Once you use it on a trip, going back to a fixed screen feels noticeably more limiting.

3. Dual Lenses, Zoom & Low-Light Performance

Having native 1x and 3x lenses on a pocket-sized gimbal is huge for hotel walkthroughs — letting you capture tight villas and sweeping ocean views on the wide end, then punch into architectural details, pools, or wildlife without walking right up to them. We pushed both cameras across Night Mode on 1x and 3x, all the way up to 12x zoom, and compared 8K vs. 4K resolution and log color profiles. The Luna Ultra’s 8K headroom gives you extra cropping flexibility in post, while the Pocket 4P keeps workflow fast and delivers crisp 4K footage with impressive dynamic range.

4. Build Quality, Bundles & Wireless Mics

Side by side, the Luna Ultra definitely leans into its quirky, slightly chunkier “Wall-E” look because of its modular hardware, whereas DJI keeps things sleek, familiar, and effortless to slip into a pocket. Looking closely at what you actually get in the box — comparing the DJI Vlog Combo vs. Insta360 Creator Combo, plus pairing them with the DJI Mic Mini 2 vs. Insta360 Mic Pro — DJI makes a very strong argument on overall price and value, especially if you already shoot in the DJI ecosystem.

5. Final Verdict: Which One Earned the Spot in My Travel Bag?

Both cameras are genuinely impressive upgrades for 2026 travel creators, and you won’t regret either. If you want the cleanest value for money and a no-fuss pocket gimbal that just works out of the box, the DJI Osmo Pocket 4P is tough to beat.

For my day-to-day hotel tours and travel vlogging, though, the Insta360 Luna Ultra is the camera that stayed in my bag — and it’s the setup we’ve filmed our newest Bali, Sardinia, and Maldives episodes on ever since. Pair it with the Wide Angle Lens (a must for tight hotel rooms and bathrooms) and the Battery Handle for full-day resort shoots, and it replaces a much heavier camera kit.
